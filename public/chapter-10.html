CHAPTER 10: WLAN AND IP NETWORK RISK ASSESSMENT

SECTION 1: RISK ASSESSMENT OVERVIEW

🔑 WHAT IS RISK ASSESSMENT? (NIST DEFINITION)

Definition: The process of identifying, estimating, and prioritizing risks to organizational operations (including mission, functions, image, reputation), organizational assets, individuals, other organizations, and the nation, resulting from the operation of an information system.

The 5 Assessment Components:

ComponentDescription
1. AssetsAll assets related to the system or network.
2. Known ThreatsThreats that could affect the security of the system or network.
3. VulnerabilitiesSystem or network vulnerabilities.
4. Potential ImpactsImpacts and levels of risk associated with threats.
5. Required MeasuresMeasures to mitigate the vulnerability and threat.

Memory Aid:

"A-T-V-I-M" — Assets, Threats, Vulnerabilities, Impacts, Measures.

🔑 SYSTEM OR NETWORK VULNERABILITIES (THE 3 CATEGORIES)

VulnerabilityDescriptionWhy It Matters
InterceptionData can be intercepted, stolen, or modified.Results in loss of confidentiality and integrity.
(Un)AvailabilitySystem, servers, and applications must be available for their purpose under Service Level Agreements (SLAs).IP mobility and remote access make this harder as more workers are outside the office.
AccessPoints where remote/external users can join the network and internal users communicate to the outside world (email, IM, internet).These are the most vulnerable locations in a network.

Memory Aid:

"I-A-A" — Interception, Availability, Access.

🔑 SOLUTIONS FOR SYSTEM AND NETWORK VULNERABILITIES

VulnerabilitySolutionDetails
InterceptionEncryptionEncrypt sensitive payloads (TLS, IPsec, WPA2/WPA3). Note: Does not necessarily hide all network headers or metadata.
AvailabilityFault-Tolerant / High-Availability DesignEliminate single points of failure. Ensure alternative path, server, or app for uninterrupted service.
Access ControlFirewalls, IPS, SSL/VPN, DMZAct as gateways into and out of the network. Integrate authentication, authorization, and filtering of incoming protocols/activity.

Real-World Example:

Think of Interception like someone reading your postcard.

Think of Availability like having a backup generator when the power goes out.

Think of Access Control like a bouncer checking IDs at the club door.

🔑 INPUTS TO THE RISK ASSESSMENT PROCESS

Definition: To identify and address all network vulnerabilities, certain inputs are required.

Key Inputs:

  • Network inventories.
  • Network diagrams.
  • Network designs.
  • Understanding of all protocols and requirements.
  • Gathering information through collaborating with developers, vendors, and interviews.

Key Considerations:

  • Web apps/servers: Add many more vulnerabilities in real-world scenarios.
  • Vulnerability Scanners (e.g., Nessus): Expose thousands of known OS vulnerabilities.
  • Continuous Process: Security assessment should be conducted periodically (based on risk/compliance), after significant changes/incidents, or when new technologies are introduced.
  • BYOA (Bring Your Own Application): Cloud apps (Dropbox, Google Docs) introduce new threats → fresh vulnerability scan required.
  • Follow-up: A security audit is a typical must-do after the assessment.
SECTION 2: RISK ASSESSMENT ON WLANS

🔑 COMMON WLAN RISKS

Definition: It is critical to identify how a risk assessment on a WLAN differs from traditional wired networks.

The 6 Most Common WLAN Risks:

RiskDescription
1. Exposure to SniffersCritical information exposed to wireless sniffers and eavesdroppers.
2. LeakageCritical information leaked beyond network boundaries on unsecured devices.
3. Theft of DataData stolen.
4. Physical CapturingLoss, theft, or hijacking of a mobile device.
5. FraudCaused by disruption, eavesdropping, and copying of data.
6. MalwareViruses, worms, and Trojans for WLANs.

Memory Aid:

"Sniff, Leak, Steal, Capture, Fraud, Malware."

🔑 COMMON MITIGATION ELEMENTS (WLAN & WIRED)

Definition: Elements to mitigate risks associated with both WLANs and wired networks.

Mitigation ElementDescription
Access ControlRestrict who can connect.
User AuthenticationVerify identity.
Data EncryptionProtect data in transit.
Intrusion PreventionBlock malicious traffic.
Antivirus & AntimalwareProtect endpoints.
ComplianceAdhere to standards, guidelines, and policies.
Network-Perimeter & Internet SecurityThe network perimeter is the edge of the internal network. Note: Cloud computing and IoT have eroded the perimeter.
Transmission SecurityNon-cryptographic methods to avoid interception, detection, and anti-jamming (e.g., spread spectrum, frequency hopping). Important: These improve interference resilience but DO NOT replace encryption and authentication.
Application & Web ServicesSecure the apps themselves.

"A-U-D-I-A-C-P-T-A" — Access, User auth, Data encryption, Intrusion prevention, Antivirus, Compliance, Perimeter, Transmission, Application.

SECTION 4: IT SECURITY MANAGEMENT & LEGAL REQUIREMENTS

🔑 IT SECURITY MANAGEMENT

Definition: Performed as part of a cycle of repeating security processes. Security practices must be cost-effective according to the size and value of assets.

The 2 Logical Steps:

  1. Identify the assets needing protection, and their relative value and importance.
  1. Identify the threats to those assets and the possible consequences.

Methodology:

MethodDescriptionOutput
Quantitative AssessmentAssigns a monetary value to the assets.Quantitative statement on risk and the cost of impact.
Qualitative AssessmentA more subjective calculation of risk by assigning it a level and probability multiplier.Risk and impact level (High/Medium/Low).

🔑 LEGAL REQUIREMENTS & JUSTIFICATIONS

Legal Requirements:

  • Laws and regulations require periodic risk assessments.
  • U.S. Examples: Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) and Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA).
  • No specific methods are required, but effective and reasonable security must be proved to an independent auditor.

Other Justifications for Risk Assessments:

JustificationDescription
Threat IdentificationIdentifies threats to valuable company assets.
Management AwarenessRaises awareness within management of the required costs to protect systems and services.
Interdepartmental CollaborationRaises awareness of security & requirements. Enables non-technology departments to provide input and revise work practices (a form of self-auditing).
GoalIdentify vulnerabilities, prioritize, apply quantitative/qualitative values to possible impacts, and establish a cost-effective security program to present to management.

Key Point: Risk assessments vary in scope. A small company may follow the framework but won't cover the full scope or require full diligence.

SECTION 5: SECURITY RISK ASSESSMENT (SRA) STAGES

🔑 THE 5 SRA STAGES

Definition: The risk assessment process follows 5 distinct stages.

The Stages:

StageNameDescription
1PlanningDetermine stakeholders, roles, responsibilities. Seek, grant, and document permission.
2Information GatheringUnderstand existing systems, network, and environment.
3Risk AnalysisAnalyze threats, vulnerabilities, impacts, and probabilities.
4Identifying & Implementing ControlsSelect and implement safeguards.
5MonitoringContinuously monitor and repeat the process.

Conceptual View:

Risk = Likelihood × Impact (Depends on threats, vulnerabilities, asset value, likelihood, impact, existing controls.)

Document the Assessment: Record assumptions, supporting evidence, risk owners, and decisions so the assessment can be reviewed and used as a baseline.

Memory Aid:

"P-I-R-C-M" — Planning, Info Gathering, Risk Analysis, Controls, Monitoring.

🔑 STAGE 1 & 2: PLANNING & INFORMATION GATHERING

Planning:

  • Occurs before any assessment can take place.
  • Determine stakeholders, roles, responsibilities.
  • Permission is sought, granted, and documented.

Information Gathering:

  • Objective: Not just to collect information, but to understand the existing systems, network, and environment.

What is Gathered:

CategoryExamples
Security PoliciesSecurity policy and objectives, documented policies and guidelines.
ArchitectureSystem and network architecture, as-built designs and diagrams.
Physical AssetsPhysical assets and inventories.
Network DetailsNetwork protocols and services, firewall deployment and policy.
Access ControlAccess control procedures, identification and authorization mechanisms.

🔑 STAGE 2 (CONT.): GENERAL CONTROL REVIEW (GCR)

Definition: Identifies threats existing in the general security processes. Enforced to check whether physical security measures match policy and documentation.

How It's Done:

  • Physical access control visibly noted.
  • Policy and procedures checked for compliance via group meetings or multilevel interviews.
  • Auditors conduct documentation reviews, site visits (data centers, computer rooms), and stakeholder interviews. Multilevel interviews can effectively prove a failure to implement security policy.

What GCR is Concerned With (High-Level Functions):

  • Physical security.
  • Change-management control.
  • Access control/authentication and authorization.
  • Awareness program and training.
  • Roles and responsibilities.
  • Security policy, guidelines, standards, work practices, and procedures.

Memory Aid:

"Physical, Change, Access, Awareness, Roles, Policy."

🔑 STAGE 2 (CONT.): SYSTEM REVIEW

Definition: Looks at server logs and system files for baseline vulnerabilities.

What is Considered:

  • Access control files.
  • Running services and processes.
  • Configuration settings.
  • Security patch levels.
  • Encryption and authentication tools.
  • Network management tools.
  • Logging or intrusion detection tools.
  • Vulnerability scanners.

What It Spots:

  • Abnormal behavior (e.g., repeated unsuccessful logins on a secure account like admin/root).

Key Point: Specialized tools automate data collection, greatly reducing the time taken to collect configuration files manually.

🔑 STAGE 3: RISK ANALYSIS — ASSET IDENTIFICATION & THREAT ANALYSIS

Asset Identification and Valuation:

  • Classify as process, application, server, router, or information.
  • Place a value on the asset in terms of:
Value TypeExamples
Tangible ValuesCost of replacing physical devices.
Intangible ValuesReputation or lost data.
Information ValuesName and type of information, information flows (incoming/outgoing).
Asset DetailsPhysical location, software installed, indicators of importance/services.
Assigned ValuesValues assigned to each individual asset.

Threat Analysis (3 Types):

Threat TypeDescription
Social ThreatsE.g., social engineering, phishing.
Technical ThreatsE.g., malware, exploits.
Environmental ThreatsE.g., power outages, natural disasters.

Memory Aid:

"S-T-E" — Social, Technical, Environmental threats.

🔑 STAGE 3 (CONT.): VULNERABILITY ASSESSMENT

Definition: Assess weakness/gap in the defense of an asset that makes it a potential target for exploitation.

Examples of Vulnerabilities:

  • Missing security patches.
  • Poor awareness programs.
  • Environmental factors (e.g., poor regional electric supply).

Key Points:

  • Involves identifying, evaluating, and documenting possible asset vulnerabilities.
  • Measured in terms of accessibility and number of authorized users. The threat landscape increases in DMZs (like web servers).
  • Vulnerability Scanners (VS): Used to identify missing patches/gaps.
    • Uses databases of thousands of known vulnerabilities.
    • Version Analysis: Sends requests and analyzes headers for version details.
    • Behavior Analysis: Relies on how the system responds to requests (unexpected responses).
    • Example: OpenVAS 7.0 (from Nessus project) is updated daily and tests for 35,000 vulnerabilities.
    • False Positives: Possible (e.g., an uploaded version may activate after a reboot).

🔑 STAGE 3 (CONT.): MAPPING, IMPACT & PROBABILITY ASSESSMENT

Asset, Threat, and Vulnerability Mapping:

  • Establish a one-to-one relationship.
  • May be undesirable when assessing likelihood at the business function level.

Impact and Probability Assessment:

AssessmentDefinitionKey Factors
Impact AssessmentDetermines the consequences (degree of harm) of a threat exploiting a vulnerability.Availability of service, loss of reputation, profit loss, increased costs, drops in performance. The more severe the potential impact, the greater the risk and need to address it first.
Probability (Likelihood) AssessmentIdentifies greater or lesser threats based on a score rather than a mathematical probability.Expressed as high, medium, or low based on: ability/motivation of threat source, nature of vulnerability, effectiveness of security controls.

🔑 STAGE 3 (CONT.): RISK RESULT ANALYSIS (QUALITATIVE & QUANTITATIVE)

Qualitative Methods:

  • Rely on an assessor's subjective assumptions (experience, research, history, judgment).
  • Descriptive: expressed in words and rankings of severity.
  • Used during high-level assessments (prior to planning) and repeating enterprise assessments where there is no time/budget for quantitative methods.

Quantitative Methods:

  • Expressed in financial worth.
  • SLE (Single Loss Expectancy): Expected monetary cost of occurrence of a risk on an asset.
  • ARO (Annual Rate of Occurrence): Probability that a risk will occur and how many times in a particular year, if no action is taken.
  • ALE (Annual Loss Expectancy): Product of SLE and ARO.

Risk Map: Graphically displaying assets and risk levels (impact on Y-axis, likelihood on X-axis).

Matrix Approach (Example: Analysis of Risk on Unencrypted data on a WLAN):

Risk CategoryImpactLikelihoodRisk Level
Loss of confidentiality4312
Loss of data integrity326
Loss of availability224
Overall326

🧮 QUANTITATIVE RISK: SLE AND ALE CALCULATIONS ⭐ CORE FORMULAS

The Formulas:

SLE = Asset Value (AV) × Exposure Factor (EF)ALE = SLE × Annualized Rate of Occurrence (ARO)

Where:

  • AV: Asset Value (monetary).
  • EF: Exposure Factor (percentage of asset lost in a single incident).
  • ARO: Annualized Rate of Occurrence (frequency per year).
  • SLE: Single Loss Expectancy.
  • ALE: Annual Loss Expectancy.

Example Calculation:

VariableValue
Asset Value (AV)$100,000
Exposure Factor (EF)30% (0.30)
Annualized Rate (ARO)0.5 / year

Decision Point:

  • It is appropriate to spend $5,000/year to reduce ALE to $3,000/year.

Memory Aid:

"SLE = AV × EF. ALE = SLE × ARO."

"SLE is one hit. ALE is the yearly bill."

🔑 STAGE 4: IDENTIFYING AND IMPLEMENTING CONTROLS

Definition: Select and implement appropriate controls (safeguards) to mitigate the risk for each asset, threat, and vulnerability mapping.

The 3 Types of Controls:

Control TypeDescriptionExamples
Technical ControlsHardware/software that enforces security.Access controls, antivirus software, firewalls.
Administrative/Management ControlsRules and planning.Security planning and implementing rules.
Operational ControlsDay-to-day operations.Security personnel, data backups, contingency plans, system maintenance.

Memory Aid:

"T-A-O" — Technical, Administrative, Operational.

🔑 RISK TREATMENT AND RESIDUAL RISK

Definition: Choose a response appropriate to business priorities and risk appetite.

The 4 Risk Treatment Options:

OptionDescriptionExample
AvoidEliminate the risk.Disable an unnecessary exposed guest WLAN.
MitigateReduce the risk.Use 802.1X, segmentation, and monitoring.
Transfer / ShareShift the risk to a third party.Contractually share defined financial consequences.
AcceptAcknowledge and approve the risk.Document and approve a tolerable residual risk.

Residual Risk = risk remaining after selected controls are applied.

Memory Aid:

"A-M-T-A" — Avoid, Mitigate, Transfer, Accept.

Repetitive Process:

  • Risk assessment is repetitive; each assessment is a snapshot in time.
  • Document results as a baseline for future reassessments.
  • Can be the foundation for introducing new protocols and hardware.
SECTION 6: SECURITY AUDITS

🔑 RISK ASSESSMENT vs SECURITY AUDIT

Definition: While related, they serve different purposes.

AspectRisk AssessmentSecurity Audit
PurposeIdentifies, estimates, and prioritizes risks.Looks for proof that best practices and procedures were followed.
TimingOccurs at the beginning of the cycle.A repetitive checking process that occurs throughout the cycle.

🔑 WHAT AN AUDITOR LOOKS FOR (WLAN)

The 7 Audit Checklist Items:

ItemDescription
1. Unauthorized AccessPotential for unauthorized access to the WLAN.
2. RF LeakageRF leakage beyond the physical perimeter.
3. Legacy ConfigurationsUse of obsolete/weak legacy configs (WEP, original WPA). Verify WPA2-AES/WPA3 settings.
4. Rogue APsExistence of rogue access points.
5. Pre-Shared KeysIf PSKs are used, how widely the key is known (or the sharing method).
6. Personnel KnowledgeKnowledge, training, and awareness of security personnel and staff.
7. Policy ConformityWireless network's conformity to the company's security policy.

Memory Aid:

"Access, Leakage, Legacy, Rogue, PSK, Personnel, Policy."

🔑 PRACTICAL WLAN SECURITY AUDIT CHECKLIST

Definition: Review evidence, configuration, and monitoring—not only the presence of policies.

The 7-Point Practical Checklist:

  1. Identify unauthorized / rogue access points.
  1. Verify WPA2-AES or WPA3; prohibit WEP / legacy WPA.
  1. Review 802.1X/RADIUS and credential lifecycle.
  1. Test guest / student / faculty segmentation.
  1. Check firmware, patching, and configuration baselines.
  1. Review RF coverage, logging, and incident response.

Memory Aid:

"Rogue, WPA3, 802.1X, Segmentation, Patching, RF, Logging."

✅ THE CORE TRUTH
Risk assessment is the foundation of IT security. It follows a 5-stage process (Planning, Info Gathering, Risk Analysis, Controls, Monitoring) to identify, estimate, and prioritize risks. It can be qualitative (subjective levels) or quantitative (SLE/ALE monetary values). Once risks are understood, they are treated (Avoid, Mitigate, Transfer, Accept), leaving only residual risk. Finally, security audits ensure that the chosen best practices and policies are actually being followed.
